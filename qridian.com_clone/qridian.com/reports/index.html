This document outlines the privacy-by-design framework and physical activity results from the QRIDIAN pilot held on April 18, 2026.

1. Visual: Actual Participant Density Map

  • A satellite image of Holmebrook Valley Park overlaid with a colorful heatmap.
  • Activity is most concentrated (red and orange) along the central lake paths and specific trail junctions, with green "glows" indicating broader, distributed exploration throughout the park's wooded areas and open spaces.

2. Data Ethics & "The Haversine Advantage"

  • Privacy-by-Design: The system records activity without invasive GPS tracking.
  • Methodology: It utilizes the Haversine formula (noted as "Harvestine" in the header) to calculate distances based only on "handshakes" at specific scan points.
  • Data Protection: No identity-linked profiles or movement trails are stored on servers.

3. Infrastructure & Strategic Flow

  • Offline-First Design: The QR-based Narrative Engine uses local data caching so story fragments remain accessible even in areas with poor mobile signals.
  • Behavioral Funnelling: To prevent congestion, "Spatial Stewardship" was used to assign high point yields (1,840 pts) to distant locations like "Margery Weaver" and low yields (40 pts) to entry-zone checkpoints.

4. Physical Exertion Data

  • Step Count: Participants generated approximately 158,670 steps within a 210-minute window.
  • True Intensity: Because point-to-point measurements ignore winding paths and elevation, actual physical exertion is estimated to be 15%–20% higher than the minimum recorded data.

5. The QRIDIAN Privacy Triple-Lock

  • No Accounts: Participants play without signing up or creating profiles.
  • No Tracking: Zero live GPS pings; only "handshakes" at scan points are recorded.
  • No Paper Trail: All personal data is deleted by default rather than by request.

  • Teams moved independently throughout the park to find checkpoints.

  • After gathering sufficient resources, teams accessed a digital forge station at basecamp.
  • Participants synthesized raw materials into high-value items; for example, combining Lead Pigs and Woad Pigment to create a Pewter Chalice.

4. The Market Phase | The Reveal & Negotiation

  • During the final hour, the leaderboard was revealed to all teams for the first time.
  • The focus shifted to face-to-face bartering as teams negotiated with one another to secure the materials needed for "masterpiece commissions".

5. 12:30 | The Final Tab | The Buyback

  • The event concluded at the "Institute Buyback," where teams liquidated remaining inventory for points.
  • The session ended with the final scoring reveal and digital awards, such as recognition for the team that traveled the furthest distance.
